# Addendum to London Baptist Confession of 1689

# Of Cessationism

I believe that the sign gifts during the times of the prophets, Christ, and the apostles have ceased in a normative sense. I believe that God gave the sign gifts to confirm God's anointing on prophets and apostles for the specific ministries that God gave to them, including speaking his words and messages to others. [^Add-1]

Now that the canon of Scripture is complete and closed, we no longer need sign gifts to authenticate people speaking for God. We have all we need to know God's mind by looking to his word that is contained in 66 books of the Bible. In fact, the Lord issues judgment upon anyone that adds or takes away from the prophecy of Revelation. [^Add-1a] Furthermore, Paul mentioned a time when prophecies will pass away. [^Add-1b] I believe that day has alreay occured.

This does not mean that miracles do not still happen. [^Add-1c] The greatest miracle occurs every time a dead sinner is regenerated to receive newness of life. [^Add-2] Furthermore, James exhorts that the person who is sick to ask the elders to anoint him with oil and to pray for his recovery. This activity includes the sick person confessing his sin. The intent is for the Lord to answer this obedience of faith by healing the person. [^Add-3] But in our times, I don't believe that anyone has the power to command a person to be healed like what Peter and John did when healing the lame begar [^Add-4], when Peter raised Dorcas from the dead [^Add-5], and when Paul raised Eutychus from the dead [^Add-6]. While I do believe that the Lord still miraculously heals people through answering prayer, I don't believe that every prayer for someone's recovery will be answered by the Lord miraculously heal that person. Many times God uses simple and ordinary means of his providence. Other times, he completely ignores the requests. However, when God does intervene in miraculous ways, he is not doing so to bring new revelation.

# Of Sexes and Marriage

God created male and female, two and only two sexes. God fixes a person's sex at conception (actually sooner than that -- God knows and decress all things [^Add-Genders-1]). God has designed and created each human sex to equally be his image bearers[^Add-Genders-2] while at the same time each having distinct natures to serve God's purposes [^Add-Genders-3], normatively in a one flesh relationship as one husband and one wife [^Add-Genders-4] to fulfill God's purposes for them to exercise dominion over and fill the earth. # Of the Importance of the Early Chapters in Genesis

I believe that much of the breakdown in the West is traceable to a low view and ignorance of the opening chapters in Genesis. The West is seeing the effects of a long march away from Biblical truth that has been heavily influenced by the Enlightenment. Many thought leaders of the 20th century wanted us to believe that we can only know truth through what can objectively measure, quantify, and observe. Human reason was the guiding principle that would eventually lead us to a utopian ideal. Sadly, but not surprising, the 21st century is marked by wars and rumors of wars, pandemics (some made by scientists, the high priests of our day), and all sorts of fearmongering. Factoring in the two world wars of the previous century, all of the other global conflicts, and societal genocide, it has been suggested that the 1900s was the bloodiest century in the history of human civilization.

Postmodernism has further corrupted the foundations of civilization by redefining terms that mean entirely different things than they meant for millinea. In the West, by and large, marriage used to be between a man and a woman. Today, there are those that suggest you can be married to someone of the same gender, to an inanimate object, or to all kinds of polyamorous combinations. The postmodernist demands that it is up to the individual to determine their truth regarding marriage. By attacking a foundational building block of society, the home, postmoderns are dismantling civilization as we know it. Similarly, it used to be commonly accepted that there were only two genders immutability assigned at birth, male and female. Today, the zeitgeist argues that gender is a social construct that is open to the whims and desires of the individual. Not only has this lead to people trying to reassign their gender but constructing new and bizarre genders out of thin air. This has led to all kinds of confusion over pronoun usage and the construction of more pronouns than probably anyone can name. After all, they are constantly changing with new ones being regularly added. What's worse is that the some of the most vulnerable members of society, children, have been given puberty blockers and gender reassignment surgeries. There is no small irony that when marriage can mean anything, it now means nothing, i.e., some believe it can be whatever you want it to be to suite your sinful proclivities. When gender can mean anything, it now means nothing, i.e., whatever you want it to be in order to be true to yourself.

Where we are today, didn't appear out of nowhere. Ideas have consequences. Sometimes the fruit of those consequences do not appear and are not reaped until the next and following generations. Therefore, getting to the root of the problem requires rediscovering Biblical truth. Our times require us to know and understand how God designed the world and how he intends it to function. Furthermore, by tracing ideas back to their source or seed form, we can more clearly analyze them in light of the Bible to address the underlying sinful thoughts that led to current sinful manifestations. Until we expose ourselves to the Bible in that way, we can never hope to truly address the ills of our time.

So how does that relate to the opening of Genesis? Genesis 1-2 describes the Almighty God as creating the world from nothing. He, through His power and infinite wisdom, formed everything by the spoken word whereby the Son carried out his Father's command through the power of the Holy Spirit. As Creator, he made everything perfectly. There was no sin and no evil. It was very good. God made man and woman as only two distinct genders, equal as image bearers of God yet with distinct roles and characteristics for obeying his mandates to exercise dominion. God instituted the marriage between one man and one woman for life. Each family unit is a building block in the mandate to exercise dominion.

Because sin entered the world, death entered -- not through some evolutionary process of repetitive struggle and "refinement" that violates the Second Law of Thermodynamics. Man's sin has subjected all creation to travail, groaning, and death. But God, who is rich in mercy, promised the deliverer through the woman's offspring who would deliver us from the deceitfulness of Satan and the bondage of our wills. God presents himself as the just judge of sinners while also being a merciful deliverer.

In Genesis 4 we learn that the object of our worship is either the idol of self and doubts about God and his ways or the only true God of Scripture. Individuals either believe self or believe God. Only those that seek after God are those that demonstrate they have a knowledge of him. Genesis 6 demonstrates that it didn't take long for the post-Fall world to become a monolithic culture of debauchery and licentiousness. Thankfully, God's gracious nature extended grace to Noah and delivered a remnant while destroying the rest of man in a global flod. The flood highlights again that God is just and the justifier of those that trust in him for deliverance. The ark foreshadows the ark of safety that is only in Christ whereby he propitiated God's wrath and delivers every sinner that repents and trusts in him for deliverance.

All that to say this: if we took the first part of Genesis more seriously, we would know the following:

* God created everything from nothing
* God created man and woman
  * As equals as image bearers
  * Only two distinct genders with overlapping roles and characteristics while at the same time unique roles and characteristics of each gender
* God's creation was very good -- nothing about it was bad
* God created marriage between one man and one woman for life
* Marriage is a foundational building block of society. The command to exercise dominion and replenish the earth was for the married couple, Adam and Eve
* Work is good and commanded by God
* Rest is good and is a gift given by God for spending dedicated time to enjoy him
* Sin significantly marred God's good creation
* Death is inevitable for everyone because all have sinned, therefore, man needs to be saved
* God is just and merciful
* God promises a deliverer
* We are worshippers at heart. The question is whether it is false idol worship or the true worship of God
* Those that are righteous seek God
* We are depraved. Left to ourselves, society unravels. Sin destroys families, societies, and the world. But God's grace brings hope and deliverance
* Only those that repudiate self trust and run to God's appointed means of deliverance will be delivered from the wrath to come

How much of the core issues of our time would be diagnosed and addressed if we understood and believed the opening chapters of Genesis at face value? How can one understand the gospel and appreciate it without knowing and understanding Genesis 1-9:17?

# Of the Sabbath (the Lord's Day)

* Part of the Created Order -- built into creation; not an appendage to God's created order but baked into the very fabric of creation
* Jesus clarified that the Sabbath was created for man, not man for the Sabbath
  * God's gift to us
  * Immediately after creating man, God creates a day of rest for man to devote undistracted time to enjoying and fellowshipping with him
  * The Lord's Day, being the first day of the week, we begin our week by enjoying the Lord. Adam began his life enjoying his creator. We have the privilge to begin every week enjoying our Lord before we work for him throughout the week
* If the 10 commandments are God's moral law, how can the Sabbath be dismissed as not required for today?
  * If God is a God of rest (and work), how do we exhibit that aspect of his character as being created in his image?
  * Does the New Covenant do away with this created order or simply repurpose it to another day and heighten the celebration by giving us a day to celebrate the One who is our Sabbath rest?
* The Lord's Day changes the day of rest for the believer
  * The early church set a precedent for this practice
    * Choosing the first day of the week coincided with the pivotal event of the church, the resurrection of Jesus Christ. It was the day that they practiced communion, the breaking of the bread together (Acts 20:7). The same word used to describe Paul preaching in the synagogue (Acts 18:4, 19:8) was used to reference what Paul did on the first day of the week (Acts 20:7) ([[bkrencbib]] | Baker Encyclopedia of the Bible)
    * A regular cadence pragmatically synchronizes everyone on a schedule so there is no doubt when we the church meets. It provides a practical way for everyone to prioritize that gathering
    * This practice has remained in the church proper for a couple of thousand of years, basically from the beginning
    * A regular cadence of worship needed to be established (Hebrews refers to not forsaking the assembling of yoursleves). There is an expectation that the church meets regularly for teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayers (Acts 2:42). Colossians and Ephesians exhorts us to sing to one another which requires being gathered together
  * Christ is our Sabbath rest (Hebrews)
    * A sense that he fulfills all of our work. He certainly fulfills our need to do all of God's commands
    * A sense of anticipation of our eternal rest where we are given glorified bodies no longer encumbered by the sin and its effects
    * Yet the practice of a Lord's Day 
  * The Lord's Day maintains the created order of one day in seven
    * How much harm do we do to our spiritual life by ignoring a dedicated day of focusing on Christ our rest
    * How much harm do we do to the rest of our being? What's the wear and tear on our bodies, mind, and emotions?
    * What's the wear on our society by ignoring God's created order?
    * What spiritual toll has been exacted on our society by ignoring this?
  * Specifics
    * I'm not as convinced about avoiding all forms of recreation. Properly recreating and in the healthy amounts, can be something that recharge a person and can contribute to delighting in the God that gives us all good things to enjoy. On the other hand, recreating for the wrong reasons and inordinate amounts could distract us from growing in the grace and knowledge of God and delighting in him. Therefore, it seems prudent to let the priesthood of each believer and the heads of households determine what is allowable for them during the Lord's Day, assuming that they have a high view of being with God's people to worship, pray, and be taught. I am concerned when people prioritize athletics and many other non-essential activities over being with God's gathered people on the Lord's Day. That being said, there are appropriate seasons for an individual or a family to escape for a period of rest during a vacation that might keep them from attending a local assembly of believers. But even doing that for prolonged periods of time should become a concern for any child of God. At what point does "restful" and recreational disengagement indicate priorities and desires that no longer align with the priority that Christ has for his church?
    * We do well to be reminded how Jesus responded to those that accused his disciples of breaking the Sabbath by picking and eating corn. Jesus clarified that the Sabbath was made for man and not man for the Sabbath. In other words, God gave the Sabbath to us. As he rested in his works of creation, we rest in him, our creator. In Christ, we also rest in him our Redeemer. This should be the focus of observing the Lord's Day.
    * Regarding employment activities that require working on the Lord's Day, I think it is prudent to ask these types of questions.
      * Is it necessary work or can it wait to be done another day? Examples of necessary work would be some health care jobs and some farming jobs (animals need fed, watered, and milked). Even some of jobs can be flexed to be performed at times that minimizes the impact on the Lord's Day. Example of unnecessary work would be sporting events and entertainment in general.
      * Is the work under the category of "my ox being in the ditch?"
      * I think it is best for the individual and heads of household to work through the details of this. However, there would be an appropriate time for the church to confront a member that becomes lax in their gathering with the saints during the Lord's Day.
  * Objections
    * Paul's comments about letting each be convinced in his own mind regarding sabbath observances
      * Paul referred to sabbaths, plural. In the OT, there was more than the seventh day Sabbath. There were sabbaths throughout the year as part of various feasts and festivals. But those were part of the ceremonial law that find their fulfillment in Christ. 
    * The 10 commandments are not part of God's moral law
      * However, it seems obvious that all of the other 9 commands are baked into the character of God. Can God be divorced from any of those 9 commands?
      * Even God rested the seventh day of creation week thereby demonstrating that resting and delighting is part of his character
      * Jesus pulled his disciples away for a season of rest because the demands of ministry wore on them (Mark 6:30-32)
    * The Sabbath finds its fulfillment in Christ as does all the law. True, but if any parts of the law are part of God's moral law, Christ's fulfillment of the moral law doesn't change the requirement for us to obey that lawy. After all, we are made in his image to reflect his character. Keeping the law in no way justifies. Rather, being justified, sanctified, and endwelt by God's Spirit, we now have the desire and ability to do what pleases God. Because the created order of things and the character of God remain, it is appropriate and fitting that in the NT, the Sabbath shifts to the Lord's Day -- the day when Christ rose from the dead. It provides a day for delighting in him, our resurrected Lord; being exhorted by other believers through singing and fellowship; being equipped by pastor teachers to do the work of the ministry; by anticipating the future perfect rest; by preparing to serve the Lord throughout the week; by acknowledging the goodness of the Lord's design to live in the reality of his wise created order. If the Lord's Day is not the Christian Sabbath, how can the church and its leaders have any expectations for Christians to assemble on that day?
    * Frankly, I find it odd and strange that we have to work so hard to defend the Lord's Day with fellow believers. Why wouldn't we find it a delight coming from a good and wise God for our benefit and his glory? Might our antagonism towards it explain part of the decline and the weakness and lethargy of Christianity in the West?
