## Of the Sabbath (the Lord's Day)

There are several reasons it seems necessary to comment on the Sabbath. First, most likely, I would not have placed my position in my doctrinal statement if I had authored my own. But because I chose this confession, it forces the issue. Therefore, I feel a need to communicate my views on this topic in the context of Twenty First Century Western Christianity.

Second, Chapter 19 &mdash; Of the Law of God, Paragraph 3 refers to the Ten Commandments of Paragraph 2 as God's moral law. If the ten commandments are moral, their antithesis is immoral. By saying that they are moral is another way of saying that they represent God's character. If this is the case, observing any of these commands is not optional. Therefore, I want to explore why I think the Sabbath command is for our time, not just Israel.

Thirdly, I will spend a little bit of time on applying this for our day. I'll cover why I think the Lord's Day replaces the Sabbath that was observed on the seventh day.

### Seventh Day Rest Baked into the Natural Order

After God created the earth in six literal days, he rested on the seventh day. [^Add-Sabbath-1] Genesis 2:3 says that God blessed this day and made it holy. By making this day holy, God set it apart from all the rest of the days. It should not be interpreted to mean that the other days are not special and to be used for worshipping God and delighting in him. It is to say, though, that God set the seventh day apart as a unique day from all the other six days. In that day, he rested from all the work he did during creation.

Regarding the phrase, "made holy," the Reformation Study Bible says this. "The seventh day is the first thing in the Torah to which God imparts His holiness, thereby setting it apart to Himself (Ex. 20:11). It summons humankind to imitate the pattern of the King and so to confess God’s lordship and their consecration to Him. This sign of the covenant with God (Ex. 31:13, 17) is also a type of Christ (Col. 2:16, 17), and it gives promise of divine rest both now and forever (Matt. 11:28)." [^Add-Sabbath-2]

When Jesus defended his disciples picking grain in a grainfield to satisfy their hunger, Jesus went all the way back to the created order by saying to the Pharisees, “The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. So the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath.” (Mark 2:27-28) Note the precious gift that God gave to man by setting one day apart from all the others. Secondly, note that Jesus is the lord of the Sabbath. As Creator, Adam and Eve humbly worshipped their Creator and Lord on the first full day of their life. What a glorious way to begin one's existence by being enthralled with the Creator and Lord of the universe. 

### The Sabbath in the Ten Commandments

When God gave the Ten Commandments, [^Add-Sabbath-3] the command to keep the Sabbath as a holy (separate and distinct) day is the fourth commandment. [^Add-Sabbath-4] It's worth noting that God roots this command in the natural order of his creation. Exodus 20:11 states, "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested on the seventh day. Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy." The basis for God commanding his people to rest on the Sabbath is because that day has been set apart (Exodus 20:8, Genesis 2:3) and because God rested from his work on that day (Exodus 20:11, Genesis 2:2). In other words, part of being made in his image is resting one day in seven as God rested on the seventh day. God designed us to heartily work six days and take a seventh day of rest from our work just as the Lord did during the first week of creation.

When the Lord repeated the Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy, [^Add-Sabbath-5] the Lord gave another reason for the Sabbath. [^Add-Sabbath-6] Deuteronomy 5:15 says, "You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the LORD your God brought you out from there with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm. Therefore the LORD your God commanded you to keep the Sabbath day." The Sabbath was not simply to stop doing the work of the other six days, it was intended to be devoted to remembering the LORD as their deliverer and redeemer from Egyptian bondage. For Israel to properly keep the Sabbath day, it wasn't simply ceasing from certain activities, it was to positively engage in remembering their saving and covenant keeping God. This is consistent with one of the messages in Deuteronomy. It repeatedly exhorts the Israelites to remember the Lord and cautions them to not forget him. Actively remembering and seeking the Lord is not what the natural man does, rather it quickly forgets the Lord and his saving work. As sinners, we naturally seek our own ways. This is one of the reasons that God devoted one day of seven for man to worship and remember the LORD, their Creator and since the Fall, their Savior and Deliverer.

### Practical Matters for Our Context

For the above reasons, I believe that the Sabbath day is now what the early church called the Lord's Day. Instead of ending our week in a day of rest and worship, we have the privilege to begin our week with a day of rest and worship. Christ, our Sabbath Rest, has defeated the powers of darkness at the cross and through his resurrection. This fits with the salvation by faith in Christ alone. It is not of works. He has accomplished a full salvation and redemption. We cannot add or take away from his salvatory work. Through our union with him, we begin our week by celebrating our Lord that resurrected on the first day of the week. Because we are his, it adds meaning to our work and gives us the ability to please him in our work the remainder of the week. Furthermore, we begin our week anticipating our eternal rest where we will be with him forevermore. All curse from the Fall will be irradicated. We will serve him in the new heavens and the new earth. There will no longer be the daily toil of fighting our sin nature. Truly, it will be a glorious eternal rest in Christ that is unhindered by our sin nature or temptations to sin. We will always remember him for we will be with him forever. We will not forget because we will not have a disposition towards our old nature for that old nature will be forever gone.

So how do we honor the Sabbath today? Begin by assembling with God's people on the Lord's Day. Worship him in spirit and truth. Be equipped to do the work of the ministry by listening to sound preaching. Devote as much of the day as possible to delighting in Christ, your redeemer and deliverer from your bondage to sin. Invest in building the kingdom on that day through acts of service and discipleship. Enjoy the good gifts that God has given to you on that day.
