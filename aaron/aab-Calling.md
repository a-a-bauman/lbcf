# Calling of Aaron Bauman

The scriptures convey that there is an internal and an external sense of the call to be an elder. The internal sense of the call comes from the work of God's Spirit who graciously imparts spiritual gifts to all of his children according to his sovereign purposes.[^aab-call-1] God grants these gifts to his children so that they would mutually care for each other using the gifts that God has imparted to each. [^aab-call-2] A child of God does not get to choose which gift or gifts that God gives to anyone including oneself. Rather, God chooses which gifts to give to each child of his.[^aab-call-3] Secondly, the gifts are not to be used for self-service or personal attention but to benefit Christ's body which is composed of his children.[^aab-call-4] 

Whatever the gift or gifts that God gives to each child cannot help but manifest itself. God gives gifts to be used. Furthermore, he motivates his children to exercise their gifts and empowers them to do so. The issue isn't so much about trying to figure out which gifts one has but more about getting busy serving the Lord in the ways that he motivates one to do so.

One of the gifts that God gives is for shepherding and teaching.[^aab-call-5] The purpose of the gift is to equip saints to effectively minister, to help them mature, to stabilize and ground them in truth, and to assist in growth towards unity around and in Christ through love.[^aab-call-6] Another aspect of this gift is to defend the sheep against false teachers and false doctrines.[^aab-call-7]

By God's grace, he continues to motivate me to communicate his word to his people.[^aab-call-8] Furthermore, I have an increasing concern to see others come to salvation through the faithful proclamation of the gospel.[^aab-call-9] God continues to stir me to shepherd and disciple those entrusted to my care which include my wife, children, their spouses, and now my grandchildren. Carol and I frequently talk about the Bible and how to apply Scripture to arrive at wise decisions whether in our own lives or the lives of others we minister to. Even though parenting responsibilities change with adult children, we still care for their souls. It is a special joy to continue sweet fellowship with them and their individual families. It continues to be a blessing to grow with them in the grace of God as we pursue Christ together.

The other sense of calling comes from those within a church that validate God's gifting in an elder's life. The early church did that when they chose Barnabas and Saul for the work that the Holy Spirit had called them to perform.[^aab-call-10] Paul commanded Titus to appoint elders in every town.[^aab-call-11] I have had several people, including some of my children, ask me to serve in this capacity.

Finally, the Lord continues to grow me in sanctification.[^aab-call-12] There is a sense that I never feel ready to serve Christ's church in this capacity. I am a Christian that regularly fights my endwelling sin that vexes my soul. I long for perfection. However, I know that will not occur until this body of flesh puts on immortality and Christ glorifies me. I am thankful that God continues the work that he has begun in me. 

Because I simulataneously 

* Am compelled to shepherd and teach people
* Others have asked me to do so
* I still carry about my old sin nature
* The calling of the office of elder comes with great accountability

I approach this responsibility with a sense of trepidation mixed with the sense that I'm obligated to obey the Lord in this calling. Who is sufficient for these things?[^aab-call-13] If it wasn't for the knowledge that God's grace and his word is sufficient to accomplish his purposes, I wouldn't even consider this calling. All that being said, I covet your prayers that the Lord would continue to work his grace of sanctification in my life, that I would be sober and serious about faithfully studying and growing in my understanding of God's truth and applying it in wisdom to my life, and that I would faithfully communicate God's glorious truth to shepherd the hearts of others in our church, community, and my relationships.
