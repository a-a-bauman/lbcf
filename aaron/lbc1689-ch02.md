# Chapter 2 — Of God and the Holy Trinity

## Paragraph 1

The Lord our God is but one only living and true God;[^02-1] whose subsistence is in and of Himself,[^02-2] infinite in being and perfection; whose essence cannot be comprehended by any but Himself;[^02-3] a most pure spirit,[^02-4] invisible, without body, parts, or passions, who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto;[^02-5] who is immutable,[^02-6] immense,[^02-7] eternal,[^02-8] incomprehensible, almighty,[^02-9] every way infinite, most holy,[^02-10] most wise, most free, most absolute; working all things according to the counsel of His own immutable and most righteous will,[^02-11] for His own glory;[^02-12] most loving, gracious, merciful, long-suffering, abundant in goodness and truth, forgiving iniquity, transgression, and sin; the rewarder of them that diligently seek Him,[^02-13] and withal most just and terrible in His judgments,[^02-14] hating all sin,[^02-15] and who will by no means clear the guilty.[^02-16]

## Paragraph 2

God, having all life,[^02-17] glory,[^02-18] goodness,[^02-19] blessedness, in and of Himself, is alone in and unto Himself all-sufficient, not standing in need of any creature which He hath made, nor deriving any glory from them,[^02-20] but only manifesting His own glory in, by, unto, and upon them; He is the alone fountain of all being, of whom, through whom, and to whom are all things,[^02-21] and He hath most sovereign dominion over all creatures, to do by them, for them, or upon them, whatsoever Himself pleases;[^02-22] in His sight all things are open and manifest,[^02-23] His knowledge is infinite, infallible, and independent upon the creature, so as nothing is to Him contingent or uncertain;[^02-24] He is most holy in all His counsels, in all His works,[^02-25] and in all His commands; to Him is due from angels and men, whatsoever worship,[^02-26] service, or obedience, as creatures they owe unto the Creator, and whatever He is further pleased to require of them.

## Paragraph 3

In this divine and infinite Being there are three subsistences, the Father, the Word or Son, and Holy Spirit,[^02-27] of one substance, power, and eternity, each having the whole divine essence, yet the essence undivided:[^02-28] the Father is of none, neither begotten nor proceeding; the Son is eternally begotten of the Father;[^02-29] the Holy Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son;[^02-30] all infinite, without beginning, therefore but one God, who is not to be divided in nature and being, but distinguished by several peculiar relative properties and personal relations; which doctrine of the Trinity is the foundation of all our communion with God, and comfortable dependence on Him.
