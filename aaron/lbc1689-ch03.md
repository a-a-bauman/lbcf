_aab_: A clarifying comment on this chapter.

==I believe that God decrees the elect to be saved while at the same time holding people responsible for heeding the call of the gospel to repent from their sins and believe in Christ alone for salvation. While the sovereignty of God in salvation can seem to contradict the responsibility and the will of individuals to respond, in the mind of God, no contradiction exists. We are limited in our understanding of God and his ways. He is not. As created beings, we are called to believe all that the scriptures teach, even when we cannot harmonize or reconcile that which seems contradictory. Deuteronomy 29:29 teaches that the secret things of the Lord belong to him. Therefore, I believe that every person elected by God will be saved according to God's sovereign decrees and that that person will respond to the effectual call of the gospel even when that general call is given in a group of lost people which contains those that will never respond to that general call to repent and believe. Therefore, everyone everywhere is responsible for their choices, including whether they do or do not respond to the gospel.
 
 [This video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_I7xZhUPoY&ab_channel=GracetoYou) of John MacArthur addressing this issue has helped me. The questions he asks beginning around the 2:30 mark are particularly insightful. The 5:40 mark is crucial because trying to harmonize what God has not is dangerous at best and not permissible at worst.

There are two ditches to avoid when thinking about the sovereignty of God. The one ditch leads to fatalism. Fatalism discourages the bold, passionate, and regular plea to preach the gospel. Romans 10:14-15 teaches that God uses the preaching of the gospel to save sinners. Fatalism is heartless, hopeless, and devoid of compassion. It misses the clear teachings of Scripture that command us to engage the world with the gospel. Fatalism also discourages the hearers from responding to the gospel. Romans 10:14-15 also teaches that some sinners will respond in faith to the gospel after hearing it taught. An antidote for thinking that human response isn't important is found in a sermon of Jonathan Edwards. When he preached, [_Pressing into the Kingdom of Heaven_](https://www.jonathan-edwards.org/Pressing.html), he acknowledged that God needs to move on the unconverted when he said: "It is true, persons never will be thoroughly engaged in this business, unless it be by God's influence; but God influences persons by means." If you read the whole sermon, it is clear that Edwards strongly exhorted his hearers to _diligently engage_ themselves to make sure of their salvation. This call to this kind of exertion seems foreign to modern Calvinistic thinking — actually, it seems increasingly foreign to modern Western thought that has embraced a therapeutic and victimized mindset. But I digress.

The other ditch is that of thinking that the human will has the ability to respond to the gospel. Doing so adds human effort to salvation. It misunderstands the depravity of the human heart and that salvation is entirely of God (Psalm 3:8), through his grace (Ephesians 2:8-9), and from his foreordained plan from eternity past (Ephesians 1:4-5). This ditch can lead to pride, boasting, and self-righteousness. That misunderstands the utter lostness of an individual. It can also lead to despair and hopelessness for those who realize the fickleness and depravity of their own hearts. Therefore, a clear gospel proclamation must keep pointing people to the sufficiency of Christ alone to save to the uttermost all that call on him (Romans 10:13; Hebrews 7:25). It must emphasize the character of Christ that receives sinners that come to him in humility (Matthew 11:25-30).

The Christian is duty-bound to proclaim the gospel with boldness and liberality, assured that God is pleased to employ such means in the effectual calling of His elect. He is likewise constrained to exhort sinners earnestly that they harden not their hearts, nor delay seeking the kingdom of God. Nevertheless, it must be affirmed that salvation is the work of God alone; apart from His sovereign operation upon the hearts of men, no human persuasion or effort can bring about regeneration or faith. This reality obligates the believer to continually pray for the lost and be compassionate toward them, remembering that he was once in darkness. Were it not for the unmerited mercy of God, he would remain estranged from grace and without hope.==
