# Chapter 10

## Paragraph 2

This effectual call is of God's free and special grace alone, not from anything at all foreseen in man, nor from any power or agency in the creature,7 being wholly passive therein, being dead in sins and trespasses, until being quickened and renewed by the Holy Spirit;8 he is thereby enabled to answer this call, and to embrace the grace offered and conveyed in it, and that by no less power than that which raised up Christ from the dead.9

## Paragraph 3

Elect infants dying in infancy are regenerated and saved by Christ through the Spirit;10 who works when, and where, and how He pleases;11 so also are all elect persons, who are incapable of being outwardly called by the ministry of the Word.[^aab-9-1]

---

10 [John 3:3](https://ref.ly/John%203.3;esv?t=biblia),[5–6](https://ref.ly/John%203.5%E2%80%936;esv?t=biblia)  
11 [John 3:8](https://ref.ly/John%203.8;esv?t=biblia)
[^aab-9-1]: To clarify, I believe that God has special care and attention for children and those that are disadvantaged. Jesus welcomed the little children to come to him even when his own disciples did not desire them to be brought to Jesus. Jesus went so far as to say that "for to such belongs the kingdom of heaven." 
Luke 18:15 records them bringing even infants to Jesus yet the disciples did not want that to happen. But Jesus warmly received them and did not want anyone from hindering them to come to him. In verse 17, Jesus says that no one receives the kingdom of God without receiving it like a child. In other words, children, by their very nature, depend on others to care, nourish, and protect them. If adults understand that it is their responsibility to provide that for children who are completely dependent, how much more will God, the just and gracious God of the universe, provide that care and acceptance for little ones?

In Matthew 7:9-11 and Luke 11:11-13, Jesus makes the case that if an evil father knows how to give good gifts to their children that ask, how much more will the righteous heavenly Father give the gift of the Holy Spirit to those that ask.

Furthermore, Revelations 7:9-12 records redeemed people from "every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages" worshipping the Lamb. I would be surprised if all language groups have received the gospel across all time in order to believe in Christ. If not all language groups have received the gospel, then perhaps this is fulfilled by those children that died in the womb or infancy.

The Bible also has a special care for those groups that are particularly vulnerable. The end of Jonah is an example of that.

Finally, David was confident that he would go to see his infant child that died even though he was not able to bring him back from the dead. Either David misspoke, or there is hope that at least God's children being raised within God's covenant people will be taken to him when they die in infancy. 1 Corinthians 7 seems to have some applicability to this also.
